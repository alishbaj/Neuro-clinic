The 10-Minute Brain Health Checklist

Ten things every adult should know about their own brain health. Tick what you can answer today; the rest is your starting point.

Author: Alla Al-Habib, M.D., board-certified neurologistMedically reviewed by: Alla Al-Habib, M.D.Last reviewed: September 2026
  • Know my blood pressure

    High blood pressure is the most important modifiable risk factor for stroke and vascular brain injury. Know your numbers; most adults should aim for below 130/80 unless advised otherwise.

  • Exercise regularly

    Aim for at least 150 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity plus strength work. Physical activity is one of the best-studied protectors of brain function.

  • Get adequate sleep

    Seven to nine hours, on a regular schedule. Loud snoring, gasping or unrefreshing sleep should prompt a conversation about sleep apnea.

  • Review cardiovascular risk

    Cholesterol, glucose or A1c, weight and heart rhythm. What is good for the heart is, almost without exception, good for the brain.

  • Don’t smoke

    Smoking roughly doubles stroke risk and accelerates small vessel disease. Quitting at any age helps.

  • Review medications

    Some common medications quietly affect memory, balance and alertness, especially in combination. Bring the full list, including over-the-counter and supplements, to a physician review.

  • Maintain social connection

    Isolation and loneliness are associated with faster cognitive decline. Regular, meaningful contact matters.

  • Maintain cognitive activity

    Learning, reading, music, languages and work that challenges you. Treat hearing loss; it is a leading modifiable risk factor for cognitive decline.

  • Eat a brain-healthy dietary pattern

    Mediterranean-style patterns, rich in vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fish and olive oil, have the strongest evidence. Limit alcohol.

  • Have a preventive health plan

    Know your risks, set specific targets, and review them with a physician at least once a year.

This checklist is educational and is not a substitute for medical advice. Discuss your results with your physician, or book a consultation to build a personalized plan organized around the 7 Pillars of Brain Longevity™.

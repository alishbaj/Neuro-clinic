Our Approach

The history, the examination, then the test

How a neurology consultation with Dr. Al-Habib actually works — and what you can expect at each step.

Why We Practise This Way

Neurology is a diagnostic speciality

In neurology, the answer usually comes from listening carefully and examining thoroughly. Scans and studies confirm what the story and the examination already point to.

A headache that began suddenly is a different problem from one that has built over months, even when they feel similar to the person having them. Weakness that came on overnight means something different from weakness that crept in over a year. This is why a first visit spends most of its time on your history and a hands-on neurological examination — and why the right test, ordered for the right reason, is worth far more than a broad sweep of tests ordered in case something turns up.

Your First Visit

What to expect

Your first appointment is a full neurological consultation. Dr. Al-Habib will take a detailed history of your symptoms, review your medications and past medical records, and perform a neurological examination — testing strength, sensation, reflexes, coordination, balance, vision and, where relevant, memory and thinking.

At the end of the visit you should leave knowing what we think is going on, what we need to rule out, which tests are being ordered and why, and when we will see you again.

{{icon:info}}

Bring your medication list

A complete list with strength, dose and frequency — including over-the-counter medicines and supplements — is one of the most useful things you can bring. Many neurological symptoms are medication-related, and we cannot see that without the list.

A Patient-Centered Approach

You should understand your own diagnosis

Shared decision-making only works if the explanation actually lands.

Dr. Al-Habib places significant emphasis on educating patients about their own disease process. A person who understands why they are taking a medication, what it is meant to do and what to watch for is far more likely to stay on it and to catch a problem early.

That is particularly true in the two areas she focuses on. Stroke prevention depends almost entirely on what happens between appointments — blood pressure, cholesterol, atrial fibrillation, diabetes, smoking. Headache management depends on tracking patterns and triggers accurately enough to tell whether a treatment is working. In both cases you are the one holding the most important information, and the visit works best when we treat it that way.
